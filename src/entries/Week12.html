On Monday, we were all still recovering from the big drive from Lilongwe. I did my schoolwork and after I was finished, Uncle Peter helped me make a dumbbell. He used the stick that held up the snacks from Fiona’s mother’s business, but he took a ponga knife and chopped it so it would fit. Then he pounded 2 concrete stones on the ends and smashed it with a rock so they would fit on there good, and it was ready.

On Tuesday, I jumped right on and finished quickly with my schoolwork. Then I helped Dad with his work, searching up the test kits that he could get. I searched them up and read him the prices. That was a lot of fun. In the afternoon, the power went out. Luckily, we had the generator, so we turned it on, and Mom cooked a big pot of fried rice, because Dad’s colleagues Ms. Anneka and Mr. Gama were coming for dinner. It was delicious. The power finally came back on when we were going to bed.

On Wednesday, we had to drive Dad out to MUST, because he was only staying for a little while. I did my schoolwork in the car, and after not long, Dad was back. We took a new way home, so we could test out the road to the tea plantation. When we got home, the chambo man had come and brought campango. We had a delicious lunch. The new car came. Mr. Emmanuel’s wife had brought the kids, so we played and played and had lots of fun. For dinner, we had sweet potatoes with ndiwo and soya pieces.

On Thursday, Mom and Amayi went to go shopping in the new car! I did my schoolwork and Mom and Amayi also went to the awesome pork Chipiku. For lunch we had pork sausages and sweet potato fries. Then, Mom went to go pick Chiboda up from hockey practice. Amayi and Mom cooked chicken for the amayi because Bright is back in the hospital. Dad and I stayed home while they went to the hospital. Dad said that if they were not back in 10 or 15 minutes then he was going to bathe me and send me to bed. But they got back so we had sloppy joe pistolettes for dinner.

On Friday, I did my Unit 2 assessment in math. I am really proud because that marks half way through Math 5. Then I did the rest of my schoolwork. For the rest of the day, I hung out. I found a tire and some wire, so I connected the wire and tied it around the tire, and luckily it was already in a circle, so all I had to do was grab it and pull. I held the wire behind me and I ran up the hill. This tire isn't just a little car tire, it was for a big truck and it was HEAVY. I had fun doing that for a couple of minutes, going up and down the driveway until I got too tired to do it anymore. For dinner, Mom was making state fair subs, which is one of Dad’s favorites. We couldn’t make them for a while because there was no pork. Luckily, Mom finished just before ESCOM went out. We waited for a while and then we had dinner. Dad loved it.

On Saturday, was Peace’s engagement party. We went to that, and while we were there, I was feeding the cow. I was holding the mautu and the cow yanked super hard and it ripped off some of my skin. After, I also got to meet Uncle’s friend, which was pretty cool. The best part about the engagement was that it was a Ngoni engagement. The Angoni danced and I even got to talk to them a little bit. The word for king is Inkosi ya makosi, which means king of kings in the Ngoni language. Part of Good Samaritan was there too, and there was a lot of songs and dancing. It was lots of fun, but it was raining a lot so we were all huddled up under the umbrella that Chiboda had brought. I had a lot of fun. On our way home, we stopped at Krazy Chicken for dinner, because that’s what Amayi had chosen for her birthday.

On Sunday, it was a lazy day. I noticed that my toes were pink because my shoes got so wet yesterday that the dye went through into my skin. The chambo man came with mpasa and butterfish. We got the butterfish especially for dad because he LOVES butterfish. Lunch was so marvelous because I think mpasa is my favorite. It was Palm Sunday, so from the gate, I watched the parade going down our street, of a truck playing music and kids running behind and in front of it, waving their little palm fronds, singing and dancing. Later, I dug up a termite mound, looking for the queen. I didn’t find her, but I found the nursery and a bunch of eggs.
