Majete Wildlife Reserve              
                    
        

On Friday, we headed over to Majete Wildlife Reserve, with the hopes of tracking down a rhino.  The lodge offered a shuttle that would pick us up from the house, and take us to Majete.  I jumped on the opportunity to not have to drive, and also not have to pay to rent a larger vehicle and all of the fuel that it would take.  The shuttle arrived, and we were shocked.  It was an old, beat up minibus, with blacked out windows.  One of the rows of seats was missing, so we were both squished and super roomy at the same time.  Tim and Chiboda squeezed into the front with the driver.  Poor Tim was squished up against the window.  There were no seatbelts in the back, so Olivia held Matemba the whole way.  Because of the blacked out windows, we had no idea when we were going to turn or swerve or hit a pothole.  The poor bus was falling apart.  It rattled and made some scary sounding noises.    Luckily, in the 2.5 hour drive, only 1 piece fell off-the back to Gogo’s seat.  We finally made it to the lodge and checked in.  They had held dinner for us.  It was pizza night!  We enjoyed delicious wood-fired pizza before heading to the dormitory.  I had hoped that since it was off season, we would have the dorm to ourselves.  When we arrived, we found that two women with babies were also staying in the dorm with us.  We all found beds and crashed.  The babies only cried a couple of times in the night.

We woke up at 4:30 in the morning to go on our first game drive!  We had a blast.  We saw all sorts of amazing animals. We saw zebras, a giraffe family, hers of impala, guinea foul, and a bull elephant drinking. We saw a family of baboons, herds of waterbuck, and many beautiful birds.       We even saw some fungus growing out of some elephant dung. We learned that elephants don't fully digest their food, so many creatures get meals out of the dung including baboons, and-you guessed it-fungus. Fungus is amazing! On our way back to the entrance, the baboons started going crazy-making all sorts of yells and calls. We stopped and waited. It turns out, they had seen lions, and were alerting the impala and other prey animals so they could escape. Eventually, we spotted them. There were 4-5 lions walking through the bush! They were far off and behind some bushes, but they were there. Matemba was so excited! He has wanted to see mcango for so long.

When we got back from the game drive, we sat down for a delicious breakfast. We explored the grounds of the lodge, and played a game until it was time for lunch. We ate lunch and then headed out for another game drive. This afternoon, we saw even more animals-up close and personal. We saw zebra, impala, and a few water buffalo. We even saw a warthog-he ran away from us pretty quick though. Then, we came across the most amazing sight--a group of lions feasting on a fresh kill. They had killed an impala and were enjoying their meal. The lead lion was fierce. Several times, he had to fight off the others while he fed. It was an amazing experience, being so close to such powerful wild animals.    

After a while, we headed off to a specific place deep into the park, where our guide hoped to find rhinos. We were flying down the trails, and ended up startling quite a few zebras and even elephants on our trek out to the watering hole.   At this particular place, they had built a blind, so people could observe and study the animals that visited, without scaring them away. We got out of the truck and went up to peek. Unfortunately, since it is just the end of the rainy season, there is still plenty of water available throughout the park, so not many animals were congregating at the watering hole. We sat for a while and enjoyed some snacks while we enjoyed the scenery. A waterbuck did eventually pass by, but that was it, other than the small mbewa who were scurrying around, trying to get ahold of some of our groundnuts. I was surprised at how brave they were-one even almost snatched a groundnut right out of my hand before I noticed and shooed him away.

On our way back from the watering hole, it got dark. We had a spotlight and were able to find several nocturnal birds, including some really pretty owls, as we headed back to the entrance of the park.

We got back to the lodge and had a delicious dinner before heading back to the dormitory to crash. After the early morning and late night, we were exhausted. Luckily, the two women with babies had checked out earlier in the day, and we had the whole dorm to ourselves.

In the morning, we woke up early again for our last game drive. Little Mcango Boy wore his mcango outfit and was so excited. This morning was baby day. We saw so many baby animals on this game drive! We saw baby impala, a whole group of baby baboons playing, and even a baby hippo!   The baby waterbuck was my favorite.   We also ran into the lions again this morning. They were all lounging about and grooming each other. It was hard to believe that the animals that were so fierce yesterday could be so relaxed today. We also saw where an elephant had died in the river. The veterinarians had looked into it, and decided that he had been sick, and not a victim of poaching. When we arrived, the carcass was surrounded by crocodiles, but they weren't doing anything. Our guide explained that the crocodiles were smaller ones-younger ones, and that they didn't trust the elephant. They were trying to decide if the elephant was actually dead, or if it was some sort of a trap. Eventually, one got brave and started pulling on the elephant's trunk. They would soon feast. We also saw a bunch of hippos throughout the river. They are fun to watch for because they stay completely submerged in the water and you can't see them at all until they rise for air. It is like a game of whack-a-mole, trying to notice one after it silently rises, before it exhales and goes back under the water.  

After we left the river, we were driving through the bush when we came across a young elephant. We stopped to watch him for a while, and he crossed right in front of us. Then his mother emerged from the trees and crossed as well, clearly irritated that we were in her way. We watched them eating for a while. We heard a helicopter in the distance, and the elephants got visibly agitated. Our guide explained that elephants have exceptional memories. Most of the older elephants remember being transported, via helicpoter, to Majete several years back, when they were beginning to restore the park. She explained that the elephants always get upset when they hear the helicopter because it must have been a pretty scary experience for them. So Mama was pretty upset. The next thing we know, a TINY baby elephant emerged from where the other two had come from. He looked around and didn't see Mama, because our truck was blocking his view, so he trumpeted this sad, scared little squeal. Oh goodness, that was terrifying. Both Mama and big brother rushed over. Mama was super angry that we were between her and her baby. She demonstrated some threatening maneuvers and she and brother positioned themselves as a barricade, completely blocking the little one from view. Our guide said it was best to just sit and wait until they leave--the movement and noise of the vehicle, especially when they were already so upset, would only make things worse. Eventually, they left, and we were able to drive off.  

That wasn't our only scary elephant experience of the morning. We rounded a corner and went down a bit of a hill. We came across another family of elephants walking through the bush. Right as we went past, their little baby slipped while climbing the hill and fell down a bit, trumpeting in surprise. Great. The adult from this group came out into the road behind us, and watched as we slowly drove away, giving space to the baby. He was still there eyeing us suspiciously when we turned back to the front, only to come face to face with more elephants. This was a larger group crossing the road ahead of us. We watched for a minute, ejooying the young elephants as they went by, walking safely behind the big adult who had stood facing us, blocking our way. Then, a super tiny baby, who must have just been born came into the road. He could barely walk. He stumbled and fell in the road. We watched as the adults were nudging him and helping him up, only for him to take a couple steps and fall again. We wondered if maybe he was sick-the adults all seemed agitated and a couple made threatening gestures towards us. The elephant behind us from the other baby was still blocking our way. SO we sat and waited, as silently as we could. Eventually, the elephants all passed and we were able to go on our way. We had been there so long, our guide had to radio to the front saying that we would be late returning. I bet we were the first group to be held hostage by elephants that day.  

After the game drive, we enjoyed one last breakfast, before heading back to Blantyre.  Olivia and the boys went fishing while we were waiting to leave.   Emmanuel's friend had located a different vehicle and came to collect us.  This was SO much better.  WITH WINDOWS! AND SEATS! AND SEATBELTS! AND NO PIECES FALLING OFF! We were still a little tight on space, so Matemba sat in the floorboard between my legs in the third row.  Every time the police stopped the vehicle, I threw my skirt over his head and leaned over him, like I was trying to see out the front windows.  Matemba thought that was pretty strange, but I like to think it paid off when, at one stop, the police got very upset with the driver for not wearing his seatbelt.  The police chastised him "Your passengers have seatbelts on (gesturing to Tim), why don't you? Where is your seatbelt?"  I can only imagine how upset he would have been if he had seen Matemba passed out in the floorboard.  Other than that one stop, it was a pretty uneventful trip back home.  Most everyone slept, so the drive went by quickly.  I enjoyed getting to see the scenery that we missed on the way out there.  We have never been to that area of Malawi before.
