On Monday, Mom went to go pick up her friends from the airport. Mom's friends are Ms. Aimee, Ms. Lorissa, and Ms. Rae. They were on their plane to come visit us. Ms. Lorissa and Ms. Rae's plane got delayed, so Mom waited a little longer, but when they got here, Ms. Aimee came shortly after. While Mom was at the airport, I worked on my schoolwork and then hung out with Dad. It was a good thing that I didn't go with Mom, because the car was PACKED. They barely fit in the car with all of their bags. I finished my schoolwork pretty early, so when Mom's friends got here, I could help them unpack and talk to the people. When they got back, they unpacked some of their things and got ready to go to Game Haven for their first night in Malawi. Ms. Lorissa, Ms. Rae, Ms. Aimee, Mom, and Dad went first. Then Mom came back and got me and Amayi. We talked and ate and then when we got home, we went straight to bed.

On Tuesday, I woke up to the smell of mandazi. We ate and I worked on my schoolwork. Mom and her friends went zitenje shopping while I stayed here with Dad. They were gone QUITE a while before they came back. For lunch, we had zabwente and nyama khobe. Everybody loved it. Soon after they got back, Aunt Mary came and measured Ms. Aimee, Ms. Lorissa, and Ms. Rae, and they chose their zitenje they wanted to be made into clothes. Amayi went and made chambo for dinner, and they still weren't done yet, so they kept measuring. After a while, we ate dinner and went to bed again.

On Wednesday, we woke up and Amayi and her sister were making a bunch of snacks for the car for us, because we were about to go on a Road Buster. We ate scones for breakfast and then we hit the road. We were on the road to Liwonde! When we got to Kutchire Lodge, I took a picture with a wooden statue of a man with a BIG belly. I had taken the same picture when we were here last time, when we were adopting Chiboda back in 2023. We ate lunch and then went on a boat safari. We saw so many hippos and one family of hippos was super duper close to the boat. While we were looking at them, we got stuck. We all had to rock the boat and the guide and driver had to use big long poles to push ourselves out of the mud or water plants-whatever we were stuck in. On the ride, we saw some elephants and they were standing super deep in the water. I bet the water plants might be like an elephant's ice cream because they are soft, easy to eat, and probably taste great. As it got dark, we headed back in a beautiful sunset. While we were eating dinner, we got to see a wild cat hunting! (It was just a black house cat, but it caught a mouse right in front of us. I could hear it crunching the bones. We got to stay in a treehouse! We didn't go to bed right away--we sat on the khonde of the treehouse and looked at the stars. We talked about the constellations and tried to find the right ones and name them, but eventually we went to bed. There were frogs in the bathroom.

On Thursday, we woke up at 5:30 in the morning, so then we could get tea and toast before we headed out on our game drive. It was a little chilly but we still saw impala, waterbuck, baboons, and some cool hornbills. We even saw LION tracks. I was wondering where all the elephants were, because at Liwonde they have like a billion elephants. On our way back, we saw a few--2 of them were just snacking, and then there was a whole family. We even saw 1 baby and a juvenile. On our way back, we even saw kudu, which is like an impala but bigger, and it has gigantic ears like a rabbit, but more rounded, and it was a greyish brown. After our game drive, we had breakfast, and hit the road. This time, we were going to Cape Maclear. We listened to our audio book, The Boy Who Harnessed The Wind, and ate the delicious snacks that Amayi had packed for us. When we got to Cape Maclear, we went to Sangalala and everybody except me got the Kampango curry, and I got the double-trouble burger that everybody says is bigger than my head-but it doesn't look like that, to me at least. Nobody thought I was going to eat that whole burger, but I proved them ALL wrong. I ate it in a timely manner, too. After we ate, Uncle Peter came and then we talked to him and scheduled our boat drive for tomorrow. After scheduling it all, we talked for a while and went to bed.

On Friday, we ate a nice breakfast and waited for Uncle Peter to come and pick us up for our boat drive. First we saw the damage to a new lodge that was done by the water levels. It had just finished being built, when the water levels rose and flooded into the house, wore down the bricks and concrete, and one of the lodges, the roof had slid off and part of the concrete had completely come down. As we were going through, there was a sunken boat and we were sailing over what used to be a walking path up to a market. Now, that's all under water. After seeing the wrecked fishing village, we went over to go see Mozambique and feed a fish eagle. When we fed the fish eagle, we went to Thumbi Island for snorkeling. I got into the water fast because I didn't think that the water was cold. When Ms. Aimee tried to go in, she immediately stepped back out because it was too cold. She slowly inched into the water and so did Ms. Lorissa. Once they were in the water, they had so much fun. We looked at all of the beautiful fish and we took pictures of each other underwater with Ms. Aimee's underwater camera. We saw so many different types of colored fish. This time, I liked the blue fish with a red line on top of their dorsal fin. After snorkeling for a while, we went to a beach on Thumbi Island, where we had grilled chambo, ndiwo, and rice. There were some feral cats on the island and we let them eat our scraps. I think they were pretty happy. After eating, we went to feed more fish eagles. The winds were harder and the waves got much bigger. We fed the fish eagles and went swimming one last time before we headed back to the lodge. Once we got back, we went shopping, looking at the merchants' shops on the side of the road. I got a wooden carving of a baobob tree with a chameleon wrapped around it, and I thought it was spectacular. After doing our shopping, we got back to find ESCOM out. The staff's children were there, so while we were waiting for dinner, I played, danced, and taught them a bunch of games like leapfrog and freeze tag. Our dinner was butterfish, which Mom and I haven't had in a long time. Then, after talking a little bit more, we went to bed.

On Saturday, we woke up, ate a nice breakfast at the lodge, then we set out. Mom drove a little bit, then Ms. Lorissa wanted to take a shot at driving. Ms. Lorissa actually did ok. She drove slower, but we didn't hit too many potholes. After a while, Ms. Rae wanted to take a turn, so she drove the whole rest of the way until she got a speeding ticket in Dedza. After we left Dedza, we checked the petrol gauge and we had only 7 kilometers of gas left. The nearest gas station was 10 km away, and we only had 7, so we started praying. God answered our prayer and we got there, AND they had fuel. We filled ALLLLL the way up and got to Lilongwe with no more events. In Lilongwe, we ate linner at Casa Linda. Ms. Janet met us there with her son Shama. The adults discussed the plan and we switched cars with Ms. Janet. She said she would be at the hotel before us, but when we got to the hotel, we were there first. I went to bed, and didn't know that there was so much commotion right outside our door. It was like I was in another dimension. The key card had broken and Mom was stuck outside our room. She had to wait for two and a half hours for housekeeping to come and reboot her card. Finally, very late at night, Mom was finally able to get in, and she went straight to sleep.

On Sunday, we woke up early, packed our breakfast into takeaway boxes. We started the drive to Mdunga Village. Shama and I had lots of fun in the back. We joked around, played a staring game, and talked a LOT. When we got to the village, we went to visit a few people before heading over to Mr. Emmanuel's mother's house. When we arrived, all of the women were singing and we greeted a few people that were there. Then, we visited a church, the blind agogo that everybody likes, and after visiting those two, we went to the hospital and gave them mattresses. Then we went back to Mr. Emmanuel's mother's house for a delicious lunch of nsima, local chicken, ndiwo, rice, and a new food for us: cassava leaves. After lunch, Mom and her friends handed out blankets and shoes to the agogos, and clothes to the children. Shama and I were the police officers of the photo crowds. Ms. Rae had brought her picture printer and crowds of children were gathered around her. Shama and I stopped the pushing and fighting in the big crowds. After the handing out of blankets, shoes, and clothes, we headed back to the hotel. We were so tired, Mom and I had no dinner and went straight to bed.
