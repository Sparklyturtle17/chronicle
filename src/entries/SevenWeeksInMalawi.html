On Monday, I got up and Amayi went to the market. Mom was very busy fixing up screens on the windows, so there would be no more flies and mosquitos. That is going to be awesome. I worked on my schoolwork, and then after I was finished, I went to explore the water that was gushing into the gutter. Fiona and I took a leaf and put it in the river. It went fast at first, then when it went around a bend where the water was really strong, it went SUPER fast. When Amayi got back from the market, she had groundnuts (ndtedza) and a pumpkin (maungu). She washed and cleaned the maungu, then set it on the counter. Then I went outside, and found even more wormies for Dad. That is going to be good for his research because he might have a variety of worms. For lunch, we had nsima and telele (okra).

On Tuesday, Amayi had to go to a funeral, which is very sad. Right before she went, Amayi heard that the driveway was going to be torn up because the people wanted to find the pipe so they could fix up our water. If we wanted to go anywhere, we would have to wait. We rushed out of the house, faster than lightning. I packed up my schoolwork, and we went off to Chichiri Mall, which is a big mall in Blantyre. I did my schoolwork and then we went to KFC for lunch. After that, Mom had to go see Mr. Stewart for a meeting, so we went to a nice little coffee shop which also had a little restaurant. I thought it was cool because it had some really tasty stuff on the menu. The coolest of all, was a babycinno. It didn’t have any caffine–it was milk foam with cocoa sprinkled on the top. I thought that was really sweet, so kids could feel like they were all grown up and could drink coffee. I ran off to play at the restaurant. After we got back, Dad called Mom saying we had to go get him because the MUST bus had left him, but our tire was flat, and going to Thyolo was kind of a long drive. Uncle Peter took us to a place where they could put air in the tire that could hold us off until tomorrow, when we could fix the tire. After we got back from Thyolo, Mom cooked some spaghetti, and we were all very tired.

On Wednesday, Mom went to go and drop off Dad and when she came back, she cooked breakfast for us. Then Amayi told us both to go and take a nap, so we did, but only for like 20 minutes. When we woke up, I did some of my schoolwork. I only did math, reading, and Bible because I was so very tired. Chiboda had cooked up the maungu for us, so we had that for lunch. It was SUPER DUPER tasty. It tasted nothing like pumpkin pie. It had its own kind of sweetness, but it wasn’t sweet like pumpkin pie. I think I like it better than pumpkin pie. I went with Mom to go pick up Dad, and when we got home, you wouldn’t guess who was there! Junior!!! I hadn’t seen him for almost a week, so we played and we played, and he taught me a song that I am still practicing.

On Thursday, I woke up much less tired and I did my schoolwork, but Mom and Amayi wanted to go on a big shopping trip to get some mzitenges. A mzitenge is some thick cloth that women wear as long skirts. They also use them to be hats to put on their heads so they can balance things better on their head. Or they can strap a baby on their back, like the Indian boards that they use to strap a baby to their back. They went to go do that for several hours. After I finished my schoolwork, I got to play with Dad, and we had lots of fun. When they got back, we had zabuente for lunch. Zabuente is sliced potatoes fried in batter. They taste like french fries but are much more magnificent because they’re not just sliced potatoes that have been deep fried, but the batter adds a lot of flavor to them.

On Friday, Mom made sourdough biscuits with gravy. They were just like the biscuits at home! Then, I went with Mom and Amayi to the Limbe Market. It was packed with a bunch of people, and Mom got some shorts and shoe polish for Dad, and we even got malambe, which is a seed coated with a sour but tasty powder that you can suck on. Once you get down to the seed, you spit it out. It is the dried fruit of the Baobab tree. At the market, I saw vegetables, fish, and all sorts of things. It was also kind of scary because there were motorcycles, cars, and people with big sacks on their heads walking behind you and they didn’t give much of a warning. The market is similar to Walmart because it has sections for individual stuff. There is a big section for the vegetables and a section for the show stuff and the clothes, and whatever you need to look good to go to work. It has all the stuff you need, but it’s not like Walmart. It’s outdoors, there are a bunch of people who will be carrying huge bags on their heads full of corn or other things. It’s also different because cars and motorcycles drive through there. You would get arrested if you drove a motorcycle through Walmart. It is nothing like Walmart because they use Kwatcha instead of dollars. At the market, you don’t know if there will be what you want for shoes, because they might not have the color or size that you wanted, so you’d have to go to find another shoe stall. If you can’t find what you want, then you’re out of luck. At Walmart, you know they’ll have what you want because they have every kind of shoe in every size, so they can meet your needs. At Walmart, you get your stuff, then you pay after, but for this, it’s the same but you don’t go everywhere and then pay for your stuff at one big place. Instead, you pay each individual person. Instead of aisles and rows that are neatly aligned, it is just random little stalls everywhere you go. Nothing is organized. After you get out of the car, you have to walk around and find what you need. It’s not in a big building that has a parking lot right in front of it. The ground is all dirt because it is outdoors and it is very bumpy. There are steps up to go places and steps down and gutters and bridges. It felt like we were climbing a mountain because there were so many ups and downs.

After we left the market, we had to park the car again, so then we could go down a road that was very muddy but had some dry spots for us to walk on, to go get some rice. Then after we got the rice, we went to a spice emporium, and it smelled so great. We found lentils there that Amayi knows how to cook, so Aunt Olivia is going to be very happy because she likes lentils. We also saw oregano and pink salt and black salt. After the spice shop, we went to pick up Dad’s dry cleaning at the dry cleaner’s shop. Then we went to three butchery shops looking for pork. We couldn’t find any pork because all of the shops we went to were halal. I found cow livers and cow kidneys-they were amazing. I have no idea who would eat them, but I know that it is not me. Then we came home.

On Saturday, I woke up and Amayi said that Uncle Amos and Uncle Foster had found some snake eggs, and I would probably like to go see them. So I ran out there so fast, and there they were: snake eggs. After that, they were trying to fix up the driveway, so we could go outside easily again. I helped for a long time. My job that I was assigned to, was to smash the rocks with a big can that was very heavy. I was trying to break up the stones so it was a smooth, even surface to drive on. I worked for four hours. I learned how to mix concrete, smooth out the grout, and I learned how to fill grout in the cracks. After I got back in the house, I was tired and I was FILTHY, so I went to take a nice quick hot shower, which refreshed me a bit. While I was working, Amayi’s sister came to deliver Mom and Amayi’s dresses. She stayed for lunch, and the Chambo Man had come, so we had chambo and ndiwo for lunch with Amayi’s sister. Then, Dad and I got measured so Amayi’s sister can make us shirts. I was very excited to see the shirt from the mzitinge that I picked out.

On Sunday, we went to church at Good Samaritan. Then I played with my friends for a little while until it was time to go home. At home, Amayi had made more zabuentes and Junior came to play. After I ate lunch, we went on a walk to go see his house. His mom was not there, so we might try again another day. We got to see his house and part of a soccer game, where I almost wound up getting smacked in the head by the ball. Junior also showed us his school, which is a nice school. The classroom was long and skinny and Junior said his classroom holds 88 children in it. I bet it must be very loud. The classroom had brick walls and some concrete covering it so there could be a chalkboard on both ends. They had white chalk so they could write on the blackboard. It would be very hard to learn with everybody shouting and talking, or some kids crying-it would be very loud. With 88 children, I don’t think it would be very fun. I might build a contraption on the roof so I could hang and see. Then we came back home and Junior and I worked on a project until it was time for him to leave.
