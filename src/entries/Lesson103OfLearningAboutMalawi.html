This is the Malawian flag. The Malawian flag has a red rising half sun in the middle of the black, which symbolizes the beginning of freedom from the British and becoming their own country.

  1. The black symbolizes the black people that all live in Malawi.
  2. The red is for all of the people that died in the revolutionary war for Malawi.
  3. The green is for the agriculture and land because Malawi is beautiful.

Malawi’s main climate is hot and dry, except for the rainy season. It rains a lot when it is supposed to be wet. There are other climates, but this is Malawi’s main climate. There are areas in the north that are cooler because they are higher up. In the south, it’s warmer and hotter. The very south of Malawi is very swampy because that’s where the rivers go to.

Malawi has only three seasons, and they’re not very different. One of their seasons is hot and dry, another is hot and wet, and the last one is cooler but dry. In the hot and wet season, it gets very hot for Malawi, 82F. It’s not that hot, but it feels hot because the humidity from the rain all gathers up, so it feels very very hot. In the cool dry season, which is in June, it gets to about 68F. In the hot and dry season, it is usually about 80F. In December, it is hot, but for us in Texas,December is cold. This is because Malawi is in the southern hemisphere, so it is colder in July, June, and May, when it gets hot for Texas. Instead of there being a rainy or dry season in Texas, it can rain every day-you have to read the forecast. In Malawi, it doesn't rain just any day like it does at home. There is a rainy season, where it rains a bunch. During the rainy season, they can get about 11.8” per month. There was one time it rained and the gutters were getting so full, it almost looked like it would flood. The rain off of the house was pouring down and smashing into the mud, making it spurt everywhere.

FREEZIES!!!!!

Since it is so hot right now, Amayi showed us how to make the freezies. Freezies are like popsicles that are even tastier. They are made with malambe, which is the fruit of a baobab tree. What you need are a good strainer, some water and some powdered fruit drink mixes (like KoolAid), malambe flour, a pot, and tall thin plastic bags like ice pops come in. First, you take a pot and fill it up with water and let it get to a boil. After the water is at a good boil, you put in the malambe flour and let it boil for an hour, checking on it to stir it. After it is done cooling, you can take a strainer to catch any floaties in it, and pour it through a sieve, so we just have the nice juice of the boiled malambe. Then, you get your fruit drink powder and once you dump it in, you stir to make sure it is all in there, so it has a new flavor and doesn’t taste just like malambe. Malambe is sour and sweet at the same time. Then you pour the delicious malambe juice into the popsicle bags and tie a knot in them, just like tying off a balloon. Then you put them in the freezer and you can wait for several hours before the freezies are frozen. But be careful, you turn them upright and you can tear off a corner so you can suck on it. Now, enjoy!
