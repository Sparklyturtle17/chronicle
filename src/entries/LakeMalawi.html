This is a guest post by Mom. I figured that I would be able to provide more insight and detail than what our favorite 9 year old would provide.

On Friday, we went to Lake Malawi! First, we had to get diesel fuel for the Fortuner, then we set off. It was a six hour drive up to Cape Maclear. We stopped at the KIPS in Zomba for lunch. Then, we kept heading north. We finally arrived at the lodge, well after dark. We found out that there was a nation-wide power outage. ESCOM wasn’t sure what had caused it and didn’t know when power would be restored. It turns out that the lodge’s generator wasn’t functioning either. We had a lovely candlelit dinner outside, listening to the waves from the lake. Then we went to bed. We had a girls house and a boys house, which the boys loved.

We were woken up Saturday morning by the sounds of fishermen hauling in their catches, and women doing laundry and dishes on the beach. It was really interesting to watch them. There was a group of 3 boys who were only just younger than Chiboda, who were manning their own canoe, collecting their own haul of usipia fish. They joked and laughed while untangling the fish from their net, and worked with an efficiency that shows they’ve been doing this for a long time. The women did their laundry and dishes in the shallows, keeping sure to not let their plates or shorts float out with the tide. They used sand from the beach to scrub their pots and pans, and brushed their teeth with lake water. We had a lovely breakfast at a lakeside table, and a few artisans approached us to display their wares. We purchased a few things and booked a boat trip for the morning. Our boat guide was named Peter. He took us on a fun tour around the southern part of the lake. We went to the coast of one of the islands and dropped anchor for some snorkeling. Matemba was terrified at first, but once he figured out how the snorkel worked, he was HOOKED. He loved it. We threw pieces of bread in the water and all of the beautiful cichlids would swarm it to feed. They were so bright and colorful. I feel bad for the poor fish that get caught and had to spend their lives in an aquarium. Lake Malawi is home of hundreds of species of fish that are exclusive to Lake Malawi-they aren’t naturally found anywhere else in the world. They were so bright and colorful! My favorite was the yellow ones with brown spots. They looked like bananas. Matemba, Olivia, and Chiboda had a blast snorkeling. Amayi had never really swam before, so she was nervous at first. Peter did a great job helping her to the rocks and showing her how to float with the life vest. By the end of it, she was really having fun. Tim even got in the water after a while. He and Matemba had fun swimming around with the fish. Matemba couldn’t stop laughing when he accidentally managed to touch one of the fish. After snorkeling for a while, they all loaded back into the boat and we went around the side of the island to feed the fish eagles. It was so cool. We threw a small fish into the water right when Peter whistled, and the fish eagles would come swooping down from the top of the island and snatch the fish right out of the water. Matemba was mesmerized. He has always been fascinated with birds of prey. We fed several fish eagles on our way to another great swimming spot. Chiboda and Olivia climbed on some rocks, Tim, Amayi, and Matemba did some more swimming. This time, a fish came up and nibbled on Matemba’s lip. He loved how a fish came and ‘kissed’ him.

After swimming some more, we headed back to the lodge. Peter pointed out several other lodges along the lake, the Illovo company vacation house, and his house. He said that his family has lived in the area for generations. He explained how the lake is higher than it has been in 70 years. He said that his grandmother was the only one who remembers the lake being this high, and that the villagers were very concerned because it was still rising. The dams at the southern tip of the lake, that generate hydroelectric energy as the water travels to the Shire River are completely open, and the water levels are still rising. Peter pointed out to us how several properties were being damaged because the water is all the way at the buildings, and told stories about how when he was young, you could walk from one side of the village to the other, down the beach. The beach is completely gone now.

We got back to the lodge and decided to head next door to a restaurant for lunch. We had an amazing lunch on a deck over the lake. Tim had an Indian fish curry and Matemba’s burger was bigger than his head.

After we ate, we visited a few shops and got a few souvenirs. Gogo had a great conversation with a couple of painters. She loved their paintings and it was interesting to hear about their art school. Once we were done we went back to the lodge and the boys swam in the lake for a while. Matemba made a couple of new friends and they had a blast playing together in the water. When they left, Matemba spent a solid hour throwing his shoe into the water and pretending he was a fish eagle—swooping down to collect his prize, cawing all the while. It was really cute.

Once it was dark, we headed over to the restaurant for a tasty dinner, and then back to the chalets for bed.

Saturday morning, we woke up, had another delicious breakfast, and hit the road. We were hoping to be back in Blantyre in time to grab a late lunch.

We had been driving for a couple of hours, when we got a flat tire. Fortunately, we were right by a police checkpoint, so cars weren’t going too fast next to us. Olivia, Chiboda, and Tim spent a good while trying to change the tire. Matemba, Amayi, and Gogo sat under the shade of some trees. The jack was not cooperating. Chiboda was up under the car when the jack failed and the car fell. Fortunately, the tire wasn’t off the car yet, so it didn’t completely crush him. The police rushed over and we all lifted the car and someone drug Chiboda out from under the car. He was holding his head and crying. The police flagged down a couple of vehicles and a few men carried Chiboda to one, while some others set about finishing changing our tire. Amayi and I jumped in the back with Chiboda and raced off to the nearest hospital. The X-rays came back clean—nothing was broken or dislocated. He didn’t even have any swelling, it just hurt. It turns out, he saw the car coming down and slammed the back of his head into the pavement. The vehicle didn’t even touch him.

We loaded back in the car and hit the road again. We made it back to Chichiri without further incident, and enjoyed some well deserved KFC. Chiboda was still filthy from being under the car, so he even took a quick KFC shower. We finally got home and the power was out. We hung about for a bit before heading to bed early.
