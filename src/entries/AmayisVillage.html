On Monday, we went out to visit Amayi's village. First, we went to Limbe to purchase items to take to the Agogos and children, like we did in Mdunga village. We know that there are many people out in Amayi's village that could use some help, and wanted to do what we can. We got the same items as before-enough for 40 Agogos and 20 children. Then, we headed out to the village. As we approached, there were children who had been out as sentinals. They saw us and began screamkng and singing and running alongside the car with us. Some ran ahead to announce that we had finally arrived. The women also came running out to the road to sing and dance with us as we approached. Amayi jumped out of the car and joined the celebration. Everyone was so excited and happy. I could barely drive the car up the road because it was so narrow and crowded with singing and dancing women.

Once we arrived, we got pulled immediately into more singing and dancing. We had so much fun. After a while, Gogo went off to see about fitting some of the older people with reading glasses. She went through a lot of glasses-I hope they help. Meanwhile, Olivia and I continued to sing and dance along with the village. Some men and boys got involved too-drumming and singing. I didn't understand most of the songs, but there was one song that they were talking about how Asisi (Amayi) was Number 1. That was fun to see. They were so excited and proud of her that she brought Azungu out to THEIR village. I am proud of her too. She has worked so hard to get to where she is today. If she hadn't, we would have never known and come to love her as much as we do. She has not had as easy of a life as I have, and yet--she is happy and healthy and successful in her own right. I was happy to help shower praise onto her. At one point, my little friend from last time, Salome, came running up to me, shouting 'Azungu! Azungu!' She was pretty much glued to me the rest of the day.

Eventually, we began passing out the gifts we had brought. I got a ztinje and put Salome on my back so my hands could be free to pass things out. The Agogos lined up and we each put a few items into their bags, like they were trick-or-treating. They were so thankful and excited. They were so proud of their new polos (for the men) and zitinje (for the women). They just kept saying thank you. It is still mind-boggling to me to see how far such a small amount of money is able to go. Each of the goodie bags we prepared for them only cost $7.64 but is such a truly life changing gift for them. I wish people could truly understand how little it takes to make such a huge impact.

Next, we began passing out the gifts to the children. They were so thankful and excited to receive school supplies, a toothbrush/toothpaste, and a couple of small sweets. They were so eager to receive things that they did get a bit pushy-shovey, but it worked out. Once all of those things were gone, we passed out some toys that Gogo had brought. It was fun to see a child's face light up when they received a new toy.

After all of the gifts were distributed, we went into Amayi's house and had a delicious meal that had been prepared for us by her sister. We had nsima, local chicken, and beans. Salome joined us and shared my plate. I think she ate more than I did! She was so cute-she saw Annie, one of Amayi's nieces get a drink of water and started fussing and saying 'madzi, madzi!'

After we ate, we chatted with some of Amayi's relatives--her sisters and nieces, about some of their struggles. We saw how some of the iron sheets for their roofs were old and leaking. They also explained about how when it rains, the water just pools inside their homes where they are trying to sleep. I also gave a special gift to Salome. We had picked some special clothes and toys for her. She tried on one of her new dresses and looked so adorable! Then, we took a few family photos before heading back home.
