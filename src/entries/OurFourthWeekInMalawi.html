On Monday, I woke up and Mom was awake, so I put on my catcher’s gear, and Mom threw the balls at me. I was so great, that I only missed a couple. Even some of the crazy ones, I caught–jumping up into the air like a frog. I did my schoolwork fast, and quickly, so I could go out and play with my friends. We got a ball and some gloves, so we could throw. They LOVED it! I taught all of them how to throw. Instead of keeping the ball in their gloves and launching it underhand, I taught them to take the ball out of their glove to throw it. While Mom was fixing dinner, the power went out. Uncle Peter turned on the generator and we went to bed with the generator running.

On Tuesday, we had to go hunting at several stores to find a special baby formula that Mother Thandie asked us to get for the babies Bright, and Star. There is a shop that we went to where Mom mistook the sign for ChikuPiku, but it was actually Chipiku. It was a good grocery store with lots of American things. There was a bag of beans that were in the Malawian colors, and the exact same design, but in small letters, it said "Grown in the warm heart of India.” You would have thought that it said “grown in the warm heart of Africa” because we’re in Malawi. That company was deceitful because it implied that it was grown in Malawi, but it was actually grown in India. Then we finally found the baby formula, at Game, which is very fascinatingly owned by Walmart. Then I went outside to play with my friends.

On Wednesday, we woke up early so we could go to Good Samaritan, so Mom could talk to a Montessori teacher, and help set the nursery school up like a Montessori. I did my schoolwork at the playground on the merry go round. Then I went to run around with my friends. We climbed trees, raced, and we talked. Then we went to the hospital, so we could see the little baby, Bright. It was cool to see him, but he was so small. Bright is premature and has 2 holes in his heart. He is 2 weeks old, and has a twin brother named Star. After the hospital, we went to EllieB’s, which has a great pizza called the Ngoni pizza. It is made up of a bunch of meat and delicious cheese. Cheese is a special treat for us in Malawi because they don’t have much of it and it is very expensive.

On Thursday, in the morning, I lost my tooth, which was amazing. It was my first premolar on the left side. Bright got discharged from the hospital and we were all very happy about that. In the afternoon, the chambo man came, so we got some really tasty fish for lunch. For dinner, Dad and I made jambalaya but the power went out, so we had to cook in the dark on the gas stove. The power came back on before we went to bed.

On Friday, Mom made bread and gravy for breakfast, which was DELICIOUS! For my schoolwork, we laid outside on a blanket on the konde. The weather was very nice and there was a nice breeze. Mom fell asleep for a while. Amayi had gone to the market and got produce and freezie supplies. We made freezies and we were all very excited. For dinner, we went to a new restaurant called Krazy Foods. I got some regular chicken and chips, but Mom and Dad both got masala chicken, which was fried chicken drowned in the sauce, which was too spicy for Dad. After the meal, we got milkshakes! Oh boy! It was delicious! My milkshake cup even had baseballs on it! Not really. The light from the ceiling reflected on the cup and looked like a baseball.

On Saturday, we woke up and ate breakfast, but suddenly, the Chambo Man came! We got some tasty catfish and they were so big. Amayi wasn’t feeling too great, so Mom took her to the clinic and Amayi got some injections, and when they got home, Amayi said she was doing better. For lunch we had nsima and the catfish, which was tasty. After lunch, we walked up the big hill so we could see the view. Mom thought it was MAGNIFICENT. Later, I taught Junior how to swing a bat, so he could hit the ball. Junior loved it.

On Sunday, we woke up and went to Good Samaritan for church. After, Mother Thandie took Mom and Dad to go to see Bright and Star. I went to go play with Efraim. We took turns firing his slingshot, and running around. Chiboda got to spend some time with his siblings. When we got home, Junior came over and we had freezies, played checkers, read books, and practiced pitching.

Making freezies and going to Good Samaritan was lots of fun. I hope you go to Good Samaritan if you come to Malawi.
