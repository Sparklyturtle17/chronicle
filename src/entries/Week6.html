On Monday, the Chambo Man came! While Mom was cleaning the fish, Amayi came home from her visit to her parents, and found Mom cleaning the chambo. Amayi washed the produce that she brought from the market, and helped Mom with the last little bit of fish. The fish was delicious. After we ate, it started pouring down rain. After it stopped, we had to go to a Super Agent, who is a person who gives you money. We had to go to 5 of those before we found one that could give us some money. Sadly, Bright went back in the hospital. We had State Fair Subs for dinner, which were a bun with a hole cut into it so you can stuff meat and cheese inside, and whatever else you would like to put in there.

On Tuesday, we went to Immigration, which was amazingly faster than it had ever been before. There were so many people in line, that all of them were outside. Amayi said that the ones inside the gate were people who had slept there. Thankfully, we didn’t have to wait in that huge line because we didn’t need a passport. When we got home, Mom baked some churro muffins, which are rolled in cinnamon sugar with butter. They smelled just like churros. Today was Junior’s birthday, and because we had made those, we gave him one. He loved it. We climbed trees and drew pictures until it was time for my coding lesson. For dinner, we had some great fried rice that Mom made. Unfortunately the power went out, but it wasn’t too much trouble because we had the light and the gas stove. While she was cooking, it was thunderstorming hard. Dad and I were out on the konde reading the Bible. I was reading Genesis and when I read the part about “And God said ‘Let there be light.’”, a HUGE flash of lightning appeared, which was amazing. It was awesome because God showed that he listens all the time.

On Wednesday, Mom and Dad went shopping to look for biltong for Chiboda. Biltong is a Malawian version of beef jerky. I think it’s much better. Dad always gave Chiboda beef jerky when he had a good play in one of his football games, so I had the idea of getting him biltong for his rugby game. For lunch, Mom and Amayi made sausages and onion rings that were SO good. Then we went to Chiboda’s rugby game. In the middle of it, it started pouring and there were lakes on the field, which wasn’t good. Chiboda did great. In one play, he ran so fast at the kid who was almost to scoring, he ran so fast that he went around the kid and tackled him. While the game was going on, a big beetle crawled right up on me. I didn’t notice it until it reached my bottom. Then I tried to brush it off because I thought it was a grashopper or a leaf that had gotten stuck to my pants. When I brushed it, it pulled even harder, so I turned to look at it. Mom had to use her phone to pry it off of me, but it didn’t want to go, so it pushed the little spikes on its legs into my bottom and it poked me. Everyone in the bleachers could hear me screaming. Then we had to move to the other field because Chiboda had a second game over there and we wanted to watch him play. Over there, there was a big vine with a bunch of passionfruits on them. I ran up the hill to see if they were actually passionfruits, or if they were something else. They were passionfruits. While I was up there, Mom found a dead baby rat that she thought had drowned in the rain. Dad thought its house had gotten flooded so it came up and couldn’t swim, so it drowned. When we came home, we had chambo for dinner, becuase the chambo man had came in the daytime. Amayi and Mom had prepared the fish and put them in the fridge so we could have them for dinner. They were awesome.

On Thursday, in the morning, I woke up early so she threw balls to me while I caught. I did awesome–I made a few jumping catches and diving catches. After I caught, I did my math Unit 1 assessment, which is kind of like a big test to say that I can pass to unit 2. I was so proud of myself because I got it finished in less than 4 hours. That’s special because the assessment was 5 pages long, with hard math. I did awesome! I only got 4 questions wrong, but 3 were careless mistakes.

On Friday, we had butter chicken over chips (french fries) for breakfast. It was super tasty. I did my schoolwork on the konde, which was fun because it was nice weather and the birds were chirping. For lunch, Amayi made some amazing beans with cajun seasoning on them, that Mom and Dad found at a shop. It was super duper hot in the evening. For dinner, we had bbq drumsticks, worchestershire sauce green beans, and garlic butter mashed potatoes.

On Saturday, it was raining, and Mom made magnificent pancakes for breakfast. After breakfast, Mom and I went on a shopping trip and we got some of what we needed-enough so we didn’t starve that day. Unfortunately, Shoprite was closed, which made Mom really mad. When we got home, we had catfish for lunch! The catfish was awesome! It was super tasty. We were sitting on the konde after lunch, and I was sad about missing Mardi Gras. Mom had some beads and so she and Dad threw them for me, and I thought that was really sweet, because I was missing Mardi Gras so much. Finally, for dinner, Dad and I were going to cook jambalaya. As we were getting prepped, the power went out. We listened to Mardi Gras songs in the dark while we cooked, because the people back home were waking up for Mardi Gras.

On Sunday, we woke up and went to church. I wore my Mardi Gras shirt for church. The service was about having faith in God in the bad times. After church, Mom had some things to take care of at the Children’s Home, so I got to play with my friends. I got to see Star, who is Bright’s brother, and he’s also premature. He was super cute! He was waving his arm around, smiling, and grabbing my finger tight. He was super active. Bright was still at the hospital. While I was in the toddler house, I got to see Z, who is our friend from our first visit. He is so tall now! Last of all, I got to see Peace, who was the teacher for the children who were being adopted when I was there, so we could learn English fast. Peace is Mother Thandie’s daughter, the eldest of her children. After we got home from church, we discovered that the main water pipe had burst right under our driveway, and it was GUSHING up from the ground and rushing into our yard. It was at least one. dozen. gallons. per. minute. It was flowing right into a hole in the yard, and the hole didn’t fill up, the hole only got bgger. That was concerning because we thought it might cause a sink hole, but no–it was going right into our little river on the other side of the yard. We could see the hole where the water was gushing out. While we were walking back, we found some worm trails, so we dug them up. We caught some worms and put them in a bag for Dad to take to his university, MUST, which stands for Malawi University of Science and Technology. Mother Thandie had given us some chimanga (maize) from her farm. Amayi cooked it, and it was so delicious. For dinner, we had nsima, ndiwo, and breaded chicken tenders. Amayi was making the chicken tenders, and Mom was making the ndiwo. Ndiwo was Malawian, and chicken tenders were American, so it was silly to see the country switch. Spencer cooked the nsima for us.
