Today, Amayi took us to her village, Bvumbwe. We visited a tea plantation!

It was super cool. It was so big-it started from the beginning of the tea plantation, and went all the way to Mulanje mountain, which is a big big mountain that is far far away.

It looked like a simple field that you could easily overlook. You would never know it was a tea plantation-it looked just like regular plants.

If it didn’t have a sign, we wouldn’t have known it was a tea plantation. We drove past and saw a big truck in the middle of the field, and decided to turn around to see if we could have a look. We parked on the side of the road, and then walked out to the big truck, so we could see how they did all of it.

First, there were some people who came and snipped it with hedge clippers.

They had attached buckets, so when you snipped, the tea plant goes into the little basket. Then they threw it over their shoulder into a huge basket that they have strapped onto their back. When they are snipping the leaves, they only get the top ones-the new growth, because they are nice and fresh, so they can make great tea.

Once a worker comes back to the tractor, they weigh their baskets and then dump them and go back out. They get paid based on how much tea they collected.

If you did 1,000 pounds, then you would get a LOT of money. The tea leaves are then packed into bags which are thrown up onto the top of the truck.

Then they take the leaves to be processed. The leaves are smoked until they are dry, and sorted into 3 grades. The first 2 grades are the best. They are usually sold and exported and get sent to England where they drink a lot of tea. The grade 3 tea leaves are sold to Malawian companies, like Rab’s, and processed for Malawians to drink and get their flavors.

While the man was explaining the process to us, I was watching the men pack the tea leaves into the bags, and thought that would be fun. One of the men called me over to help, and I packed a tea bag, then they hauled it onto the truck.

Then, I got to clip some of the tea leaves. It was really cool watching it. When you snipped it, the tea leaves went right into the bucket attached to the hedge clippers. I thought it was great.

My favorite job was putting the tea in the bags, because you could sniff the tea as it was going by. That tea smelled pretty good.

I think I would love to be a processor of tea, and I might be one day.
