On Friday, we went to Amayi’s village. A village is smaller than a town but it is still pretty big. It is made up of people who are all relatives. Amayi’s village is made up of her grandmother and her grandmother’s sister’s descendants. It was a lot of people. Going to Amayi’s house was a bit of a challenge. We went on the Thyolo road until we got to Bvumbwe. We turned off onto a dirt road. It was on a mountain and not many people drive cars on the road, so it was really bumpy. There were rocks and awful scraping noises on the car. While we were driving, we passed a big group of people holding a cross and walking down the road. Amayi said they were going to the Catholic church all the way down by her village. They were singing and walking and carrying a cross because it was Good Friday. It was a very long drive to Amayi’s village. When we arrived, I saw a bunch of children and the adults were looking very curiously at our car. The biggest house was her parents’ house. The next biggest house was her house. There were gardens all around and other relatives’ houses.

After introductions, we went up the mountain-hill. We were going to help Amayi harvest her maize. First, we took a long walk up a mountain that she called a hill. We had to walk down first, because Amayi’s village was kind of up. So then we walked down down down, until we got to a muddy creek, with super duper fun stepping stones. Then we ascended. It was steep! Approximately 45 degrees! There was lots of tall grass and lots of stones that you couldn’t see. The path was smooth at first, but then rocky and grassy next. We went up up up up up. Amayi’s garden is at the very top of the mountain. When we arrived, a bunch of men were already there. They were her relatives They took the corn out of the ground and threw it into a pile so then we could crack it off of the stalks and de-husk it. Then we threw the de-husked corn into a bucket. The stalk you threw to a big trash pile after you were done. I think I did 40 ears of corn! Once the buckets were full, the girls carried the buckets back to the top of the mountain. There, we tossed the maize into bags. After tossing them into huge bags, we had to carry the bags all the way down the mountain. All of the men carried the big bags on our shoulders. I carried one too! Now my shoulders are sore. We carried them all the way to Amayi’s house, which is quite a ways! We had to retrace the whole path we went up and down to get to the mountain. We dropped the bags onto her konde (porch). Then, we had to get each and every kernel off of the corn cobs. That was hard, but if you got into one row first, then it was easy with your thumb. You get as close as you can to the other row, and you can pop them out pretty fast.

After a while, Amayi’s amayi made us lunch. It was delicious! We had rice, ndiwo, and a boiled egg. While we were eating, I told Amayi that it runs in her family that everybody can cook really well.

After we ate, I went right back to picking kernels off the cob. Then, she showed us that her home was at the top of the mountain. We walked behind another garden and came to the edge. The view was astounding. You could see three HUUUUUUUGE mountains. She showed us where her church was, and a very far off market that has good prices. After we got back from the view, I saw a kid trying to do a walking handstand. I am really good at those, so I walked from Amayi’s house to one of her relatives’ house on my hands. They thought that I was amazing. I can walk forwards, backwards, left, and right on my hands. Then we took a few pictures with Amayi’s relatives and left.
