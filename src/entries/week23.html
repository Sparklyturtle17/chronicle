On Monday, I woke up and did my schoolwork. At lunchtime, we had grilled cheese sandwiches with tomato soup. Then, Amayi and Mom went to Amayi's village. I finished my schoolwork and threw the baseball with Dad. I also climbed a few trees.

On Tuesday, Mom made breakfast tacos and I did my schoolwork. After I finished my schoolwork, I went outside and played. For dinner, we had chambo. While they were cooking, the power went out, but we all knew the routine. We clear the dishes that were drying on the gas stove, and Mom and Amayi light it, so they can switch right over. It's not very hard for us anymore. We were eating dinner with the lanterns Aunt Olivia gave us, when the power finally came back on.

On Wednesday, I woke up late and started on my schoolwork, we had a very late breakfast. After the time lunch would have been, Mom and Amayi went to Bvumbwe so they could sell some notebooks to people. I finished my schoolwork and played with a big metal rod that I found. I pretended that I was jousting and ran towards the big brick wall, and smashed into it. That was probably not a good idea because it hurt. For dinner, we had chips and chicken. The power was off, so we had to get the charcoal stove to start cooking. I was helping Amayi peel big fat potatoes because we were doing a lot of potatoes that day. While I was peeling them, I did a few good ones, but then I accidentally cut my finger. I was able to get the alcohol wipes myself, and then wrap my own bandaid, because it was such a tiny cut.

On Thursday, we woke up to a nice, bright morning, and had delicious butter sugar rice for breakfast. While I was doing my schoolwork, it was time for Mom to leave so she could go to lunch with Mother Thandie. She left and I kept doing my schoolwork. After I finished my schoolwork, when it was about 12:30, Dad and I kept looking at our watches and tried to determine what time Mom would be back, because Dad and I both think that lunchtime is between 11:30 and 1. I played outside for a while. When Mom got back, she had a bunch of fruit and zimbe that Mother Thandie had given us. She also had a big platter of donuts shaped like our donuts back home, and mandazi balls. I was joyful because we hadn't had mandazi in a while. After Mom got back, Mom and Chiboda went to the Creativity Performance Night at his school. Chiboda did some cool flips. We had some rice and beans that Amayi heated up. She is very smart--she put the tupperware into a pan of water with the lid still on, so they would heat up faster.

On Friday, we had Mother Thandie's mandazi, which were delicious, but not the same as Amayi's. Amayi's have more bananas in them and Mother Thandie's had a little bit more flour in them. They tasted like a cross between a mandazi and a regular donut. In the afternoon, Mom went to take Bright to an appointment. I finished my schoolwork and then I was playing outside with Fiona, Banda, and Mpimu. After Mom got back, I was playing and Chiboda was walking with Dad, when he found a little baby bird. I named it Victory. I found 4 fat worms under the big rocks next to Uncle Kenneth's garden. Mom soaked some oatmeal for it, but Victory refused to eat it. Then Mom tried the worms, and Victory refused to eat again. Because it refused all the food, I was worried it wouldn't make it through the night.

On Saturday, I woke up to Chiboda whistling outside. I ran up and down the driveway and took my round around the house. Mom brought Victory to show me--she was still alive, but still didn't want to eat. We set her in the grass to play and left her there for a while. I came out once or twice to check on her. Victory was pecking at the ground and eating something. Mom thought that she wouldn't eat because she was more grown up and didn't want to be fed like a baby anymore. Mom also showed me these foil animals where it's foam stickers that you pop out. There are little sheets of foil in different colors. You peel off a piece of a sticker to reveal some adhesive so you can get just that spot that you want with color. Then you press it down onto the foil. Once you take the foil off, it will have color on it. I made 5. I made an elephant, a tractor, a bear, and a penguin, and a chameleon. We went to lunch in town today. We left Victory outside, but before leaving, I checked on her one last time. She was on a rock next to the big willow tree. I put my hand down, and she hopped right up on to my finger and would not let go. I put her down, and we went to KFC. Because the weather was magnificent, we could sit outside and we all ordered the extra crispy chicken. It was so good. When we got back, Mom saw Victory hopping around with a bunch of other little birds. I was super duper happy that she found her family. Later, when we went to check on her, she had flown away. That made me feel great, because another baby bird is with its family, and not dead. For dinner, we had Mom's delicious chicken and dumplings. Amayi LOVED it. It was so good that everybody had seconds, and Mom had to make more dumplings. By the time I was done, I was as stuffed as a stuffed teddy bear.

On Sunday, we woke up and I took a shower and we had yogurt and granolo for breakfast before heading to church. After church, I went and swung on the swings then I talked to some people. Mom and Dad came while Chiboda and Lola were talking about the world cup and how that one of the teams is playing in College Station. We headed home and Amayi had cooked chicke, rice, and ndiwo. It was delicious. I played with my friends. After playing, I came back inside and read my book. Mom and Amayi made soya spaghetti for dinner and it was right on the spot.
