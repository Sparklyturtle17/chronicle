On Monday, we all woke up and we went to Kholo (the chicken and chips place) to get chicken and chips for lunch. After that, we loaded up the car and headed to the airport, because we were headed to Kasungu. We took a plane to Lilongwe and then we had a few problems with our bags when we had to go back and forth between the domestic and international terminals because we thought the bags should be in the international, but they were in the domestic. After the fiasco, we met Ms. Janet, and waited a while for the Fortuner. Then we went on a diesel hunt. By the time we got to the hotel, we were pooped.

On Tuesday, we woke up bright and early. We headed out to the village. It was about an hour and a half on the paved road, and then 2 hours on a very bumpy dirt road. I slept through most of it, so I didn't see much. When we arrived, a lot of ladies were there waiting for us. They started dancing and singing their welcome songs. After that, we went to their clinic. The clinic was like a little house with some beds, some cane mats that people sit on, and a few mattresses. It was sad because there were a bunch of women who had to sleep on the floor and some with their babies. In the next room, there was the maternity ward. There were 2 beds with a curtain in between and a big bucket of water. It was a hike down the mountain and then up the mountain to get water. They had no medical supplies. No bandaids, no needles, nothing. If you were sick with the flu, you would have to go back home because there aren't any beds for you to stay in. You go see the doctor, then go back home, and go back to see the doctor again the next day, or whenever you need to. After we went to the hospital, we drove to see a boy who is an orphan. He was about 12 or 13 and wants to be a bank manager when he grows up. After talking to him a little bit, we gave him a gift bag of school supplies. After seeing the boy, we went on a hike down a mountain to go see a blind agogo. He was really happy when we gave him the new shirt, because his had holes in it. He was very happy that we took the hike down there to go see him. After we went to go see him, we went back to Mr. Emmanuel's mother's house and had lunch of chicken, nsima, and kholowa (boiled sweet potato leaves). I thought it was delicious. Everybody in Malawi cooks great. Next, we went to the church so we could give out the presents. The women were dancing and singing and the agogos were excited. All of us gave a speech, including me. While the speeches were going on, I was keeping the other children entertained by doing silly things. They were all laughing. Mom, Gogo, and Aunt Olivia distributed the gifts; first to the agogos and they were very happy, then to the orphans who were afraid of the azungu, but once they saw there were sweets in the bag, they were excited. Once we distrubuted the gits, we went back to the hotel. Luckily they had kept the kitchen open, so we could order our food. Then we went straight to bed.

On Wednesday, we woke up and ate a delicious breakfast and then we headed back to the airport. When we got back to Blantryre, Mom had realized she had left the keys for the red car that we were using back in the Fortuner. We took a taxi home and then we enjoyed the afternoon with some sugarcane. We walked down the street to get some potatoes. Mom practiced carrying them on her head, and she did a pretty good job!

On Thursday, Mom, Aunt Olivia, Gogo, and Amayi went off shopping for zitinje and other things. I stayed here with Dad and we had lots of fun. When they got back, Amayi's siter came to measure Aunt Olivia so she could get some clothes made for her.

On Friday, we woke up and packed because we were going to Majete Game Reserve. While we were waiting, I made a Greek hoplon out of a tire, some wire, and I put some paper over it so I could draw my insignia. When the shuttle got here, it was a tiny wrecked minibus that looked like it has had better days. When we went in, I went in the very back with Aunt Olivia and Gogo. Mom and Amayi went in the back seats, and then Dad, Chiboda, and the driver were all cramped up in the front seats. The bus sounded like it would explode at any minute. Everything was clanging and banging and ringing around. The bus was funny. By the time we got there, it was dark, but even through the dark, we could see how pretty it was. Tonight was pizza night! We all got single pizzas for dinner. Then we went straight to bed. We were sharing our room with other people because it was a dorm. In the middle of the night, the baby was screaming and crying. I fell asleep pretty quickly after the baby was crying.

On Saturday, we woke up before the crack of dawn. We made our orders for breakfast, but we didn't eat breakfast and we headed out for our game drive. We saw lots of animals. We saw giraffes, impalas, and we also saw lions! The baboons warned the impalas to run away because they were screeching and hooting. When we got back to the restaurant, we ate breakfast and relaxed until lunch. After we ate lunch, we went on a second game drive and saw the lions even closer--munching an impala. When I saw closer, they were only a few yards ahead of us! Then we went far out on a hunt for rhinos, because that's Dad's favorite animal. But, no luck, which was sad. Then we came back, ate dinner, and went to bed.

On Sunday, we woke up early again and went on our game drive. We saw a bunch of baby animals and I thought the baby giraffe was so cute. When baby giraffes are born, they fall 6 feet to the ground. They can run like an hour after they are born, so they pretty much hit the ground running. We saw a baby waterbuck, which they can only survive near water, and we saw some baby baboons playing. We saw the lions AGAIN! They were relaxing and napping and grooming. We even saw a baby hippo. It was super cute too. We came back, ate breakfast, and headed back in a much better vehicle.
