On Monday, I was doing my schoolwork when Mom had to go take some papers out to MUST for Dad. She left and I kept doing my schoolwork until Amayi called me for lunch. We had ndiwo and rice. I was almost done with my schoolwork when there was this HUGE lightning bolt. Because the sky was super dark, it lit up the sky like the sun had come plummeting down into Earth’s atmosphere. The thunder sounded like the biggest giant of all time had just jumped on a gong. You could hear the glass in the windows rattling. It was kind of scary, too. When Mom got back, she said she saw that lightning all the way from MUST. She heard the thunder too, but it was really far away from her, so for her the thunder happened almost 2 minutes after the lightning, but for us, it happened like 1 second after the lightning. it was really close! We left some rice for Mom and Dad, because when Mom ahd gone to the hosptal, she had come home really hungry. While Mom and Dad ate, Amayi and I went outside to play. For dinner, Dad and I made jambalaya, but you can guess what happened! The power went out as soon as we started cooking. When we make jambalaya, it always goes out and never stays on!.

On Tuesday, I was really craving oatmeal for breakfast, so Mom made some oatmeal for us. It was SO good. I worked on my schoolwork and I finished early today, which was fun because it’s always great to finish early. For lunch, Mom had found real tortillas! We had the best lunch of burritos! In the afternoon, Dad had a meeting with one of his students from Malawi. After Dad’s meeting, the chambo man came! He brought some malamba so Amayi prepared it and we had it for dinner. It was so good!

On Wednesday, I woke up and began my schoolwork. After breakfast, Mom and Amayi went shopping, which took quite a while. After I finished my schoolwork, I helped Dad with all of his work. What I helped him do was super duper fun. He was looking for a kind of rat that is missing a specific gene, so then he could test some bacteriums on them. So I scrolled down looking for rabbits and rats, everything he would need. I would tell him the prices and he would calculate them up, to see how much everything he needed would cost. It was super fun.

On Thursday, Mom went to go take Bright back to the hospital. Hooray! The smaller hole in his heart has closed up completely! That was super duper good news. When Mom got back, she made gumbo!! We needed green peppers, so we got green peppers from the small market down the street. We thought it would be cool to see the sunset on the big hill. We gave Dad the green peppers so he could take them back to the house when he went for his meeting, and we went all the way up the hill with a bunch of other children. We saw houses and they are building a new house, which was cool. The sunset–it was gold and red and pink! Under the clouds there was magnificant blues and every color you could think of in the rainbow, except green.

On Friday, we went to Bvumbwe market, because it was close to Amayi’s house where we would be harvesting her maize. At the market, I carried the charcoal on my head! I was pretty impressed because this is something I am still practicing. I carried it all the way from when we got it, to the end of the market, and back to the car. At her village, we met Amayi’s nice parents. Then we harvested and harvested and harvested. It was lots of fun. Amayi’s mother made us lunch. My shoulders were sore because of carrying the big bag on my shoulders. It was super heavy. After we got home, I had enough energy to play and play and play the rest of the day. This amazed Mom because she was pooped!

On Saturday, Amayi went to church and the market. She got a lot of things for Easter. She brought back a chicken! It was huge, and alive. After waiting a little bit, I watched Uncle Peter butcher it, which was interesting. While we were butchering it, it had one whole egg inside it, and a few yolks. The chicken’s muscles were white, not red like our muscles.

On Sunday, I wore my best clothes to church for Easter. At church they even did a play! It was about Easter and Jesus being crucified. They did a really good job. It was a very fun play. After church, Mom said she was looking for me. She was not surprised to find me in the toddler house. I was holding Bright, with Star right next to me on the bed. When we got back home, Amayi had cooked EVERYTHING, with the help of her sister and Fiona’s mother. It was a big party for Easter. Uncle Amos’s family, Uncle Kenneth's family, Uncle Peter's family, and Amayi’s sister’s family were there. Uncle Foster’s family didn’t come. Mom and I made some dyed eggs! We had purple, green, lue, red, and orange. I picked blue to eat, as blue is my favorite color. When you opened it, wherever we had cracked it, there was ths wonderful spiderweb of that color. Because I had put a lot of cracks in the egg, the blue one had magnificent spiderweb marks. We had chambo, barbacue drumsticks, local chicken, mpunga (rice), telele, and ndiwo and nsima and cabbage and sobo. We had green beans, and we also had a carrot salad. We ate in the living room because it was the only place spacious enough. The children had to eat on my big mat because it would be better than them sitting in the chairs where they could drop food everywhere. It was all super delicious. I liked the cabbage the best because I’ve always liked cabbage. After we ate, all of the children played with the big beach ball that Mom had brought. We bounced it with our hands to see how many we could get. One time, all together bouncing it around, keeping it off the ground, we got to 28.

This week was the best week of all. Oh hey, it’s April!
